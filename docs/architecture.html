the mechanism

What Arto actually is

Strip away the paint-and-flag framing and Arto is an 8-state turmite: a 2D Turing machine whose tape is the level's color grid and whose head is the robot. Each tick, the machine reads two symbols — the floor color under the robot and the robot's own internal state (its "feeling," 0–7) — and looks that pair up in three 8×8 transition tables: what to paint, what to feel next, and which way to move. The puzzle is: hand-edit those three tables so the resulting walk lands on the flag. Nothing about the simulation is special-cased per level — only the tables and the starting tape differ.

The brains aren't data structures — they're pixels

brains.lua: get_brain_sprite_pos() · levels.lua: level_init_data

The single cleverest decision in this codebase: a "brain" is not a Lua table. It's one 8×8 sprite-sheet cell, read and written a pixel at a time with sget/sset. Editing Arto's brain in the UI is literally painting on the cart's graphics memory — which is also why brains render for free with the same palette as everything else. Levels get the same treatment on the map: 22 puzzles are just rectangular regions of one shared tilemap, packed at three different zoom scales.
